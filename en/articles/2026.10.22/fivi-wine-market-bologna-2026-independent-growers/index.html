FIVI wine market 2026: a thousand growers, a trolley and Bologna

From 21 to 23 November BolognaFiere hosts the 15th Mercato dei Vini FIVI: a thousand independent growers selling with no middlemen. How to get around.

22 October 2026 · 5 min read

There is a moment, around four o’clock on a Saturday afternoon in late November, when BolognaFiere stops looking like a trade fair and becomes an enormous village cellar. People with a half-full trolley, a glass in hand and a crumpled sheet of notes ask whichever grower is in front of them whether the 2022 really is more closed than the 2021. And the grower, who pruned, harvested and bottled that wine, answers. That, in the end, is the point of the Mercato dei Vini dei Vignaioli Indipendenti, the independent winegrowers’ market: removing everything that stands between the people who make wine and the people who drink it.

From 21 to 23 November 2026 the Mercato returns to Bologna for its fifteenth edition, with around a thousand growers from every Italian region, a few European delegations and a hall devoted to artisan food producers. It is not a tasting with a sampling counter and a glossy catalogue: it is a market, in the most rustic sense of the word. You taste, you chat, you buy.

Who the independent growers are

FIVI, the Federazione Italiana Vignaioli Indipendenti, was founded in 2008 and today has more than 1,800 members. The federation’s definition of itself is simple and fairly strict: an independent vignaiolo is someone who oversees the entire life of the wine, from growing the grapes to bottling and right through to selling. No grapes bought by weight from people you do not know, no bulk wine bottled by third parties with an elegant label slapped on top.

This says nothing in itself about the quality of what is in the bottle: there are extraordinary independent growers and others who are simply honest, just as there are cooperatives and large companies making excellent wines. But it says a great deal about responsibility. If the wine does not convince, there is no outside consultant oenologist, sales rep or consortium to blame. There is one person, almost always behind their own table, who took every decision.

FIVI, for that matter, is neither a guide nor a style label. Within it coexist those who farm biodynamically and those who farm conventionally, those who give their whites long macerations and those who produce metodo classico of almost Swiss precision. The common thread is the business model, not taste. And it is precisely this variety that makes the Mercato so interesting to walk through.

From Piacenza to Bologna, fifteen editions on

FIVI has organised the Mercato since 2011, and for years its home was Piacenza. There it grew, edition after edition, until success became a logistical problem: after the record numbers of 2022, the Piacenza venue was no longer big enough. In 2023 came the move to BolognaFiere, where the event found adequate floor space and far more convenient rail access. The agreement between the fair and the federation has been renewed until 2028, so Bologna looks set to remain the venue for at least a few years.

For 2026 the floor area approaches 40,000 square metres, spread over four halls. The official poster is by the illustrator Emiliano Ponzi, who worked on the theme of the vineyard and the relationship between people and the land. The federation’s president, Rita Babini, presented it as a tribute to the vine and to growers as custodians of the landscape. The programme also includes four masterclasses under the title “Think Pink. Vini e vignaioli che costruiscono speranza” (“Wines and growers building hope”).

How to get around without getting lost (or losing count)

A thousand producers cannot be visited in three days. Accept that straight away, or you will end up wandering with an empty glass and a lost expression. A few practical suggestions.

Arrive with a short list. Three or four growers you absolutely want to meet, chosen beforehand from the exhibitor list. Leave the rest to chance: the best discoveries at the Mercato almost always come from a table you stopped at by mistake.

Choose a common thread. A region you know little about, a grape, a style. Spending an afternoon solely among bottle-fermented sparkling wines, or solely among mountain reds, teaches more than a hundred random tastings.

Spit. There is nothing snobbish about it: it is the only way to reach the thirtieth table with a clear head and remember what you liked at the fifth.

The trolley is your ally. Here you buy directly from the producer, and bottles are heavy. Trolleys and carts are available at the fair to take your purchases to the car park, and there is a shipping service to anywhere in Italy for those who overdo it, which at the Mercato happens with some regularity. Before loading six cases into the car, seriously consider shipping.

Take notes. Even just the name of the estate and a single word. The following Monday, faced with the receipt, you will find them very useful.

Go on Monday, if you can. Saturday and Sunday are the busiest days. Monday, with its earlier closing, is often the day when you can really talk to the producers.

If you are following from home

Those who cannot get to Bologna can still do the Mercato their own way. The simplest exercise: that evening, open a bottle from a small producer who does everything themselves, from vineyard to label. Ideally from an area you do not often visit, a red from the Apennines, a white from the minor islands, a hill wine that never makes it onto supermarket shelves. Then read the label right to the end: address, family name, perhaps the number of bottles produced. That is where wine stops being a product and becomes a piece of work again.

Why it matters

One could argue at length about how much the “independent” label really means to the drinker. But the FIVI Mercato answers with facts a question the wine world tends to dodge: who made this wine, and what do they think of it? In a sector where stories are often written by marketing departments, three days in which the stories are told by people with soil on their hands are worth the journey, and the trolley-induced backache too. Go with curiosity, not a shopping list. The list, in the end, writes itself.

Practical information

  • When: from Saturday 21 to Monday 23 November 2026. Saturday and Sunday from 11 to 19, Monday from 11 to 17.
  • Where: BolognaFiere, viale della Fiera 20, Bologna. Access via the Piazza Costituzione entrance and the North Entrance.
  • Exhibitors: around a thousand independent Italian growers, European delegations and a hall of artisan food producers.
  • Services: trolleys and carts for purchases, shipping service throughout Italy, ample parking near the fair, Trenitalia discounts for those arriving by train.
  • Tickets: on advance sale online on the official website.
  • Official website: mercatodeivini.it
