There is one infallible way to tell whether a winery thinks big: look at what it does with money the winery does not need. Giorgio Lungarotti, in Torgiano, built a museum. This is the seventh portrait in the "Italian Excellence" series, and the first devoted to a region that for a long time, on the map of Italian wine, was mostly an empty space between Tuscany and everything else.

## A pioneer in a region without a name

Giorgio Lungarotti, born in 1910 into a family of landowners, is the man who turned a farm in Torgiano, a few kilometres from Perugia, into a modern winery. The house dates the start of this story to the early 1960s. Umbrian wine, at the time, was in most people's minds little more than Orvieto: little was known of its reds, and even less outside the region.

Lungarotti chose to bet on Sangiovese and on the idea of an appellation. In 1968 Torgiano became a DOC, the first in Umbria, for reds and whites; in 1990 Torgiano again obtained the region's first DOCG, for Torgiano Rosso Riserva. The Region's tourism portal, [Umbria Tourism](https://www.umbriatourism.it/en/w/vini-doc-e-docg-torgiano), credits the early DOC to Lungarotti's foresight. An appellation built around one producer has its limits, and it is fair to say so; but without that push Torgiano would probably have remained a hill town like many others.

## Rubesco and Vigna Monticchio

The wine that made [Lungarotti](https://lungarotti.it/) known worldwide is called Rubesco, from the Latin for "to blush": a Sangiovese-based Torgiano Rosso, bright ruby, which by 1983 already appeared in Hugh Johnson's Modern Encyclopedia of Wine. Its most ambitious version is Rubesco Riserva Vigna Monticchio, a Torgiano Rosso Riserva DOCG made from Sangiovese alone, a wine that lasts a long time in bottle and explains better than any speech why the DOCG arrived precisely here.

Alongside the reds there is a white that played a similar role, Torre di Giano, and there is also Montefalco, where the family has a second estate. We wrote about Sagrantino and its tannic power in [Sagrantino di Montefalco](/en/articles/2026.11.06/montefalco-sagrantino-tannic-red/).

## The museum as a statement

On 23 April 1974 Giorgio Lungarotti and his wife Maria Grazia Marchetti, an art historian, opened the [Wine Museum](https://muvit.it/), MUVIT, in Torgiano's Palazzo Graziani-Baglioni. Twenty rooms tell the story of wine from the Bronze Age to late antiquity, with amphorae and symposium vessels, then tools, presses, Renaissance ceramics, prints and drawings from the fifteenth to the twentieth century. In 2000 came the Museum of the Olive and Oil, MOO; a Lungarotti Foundation has existed since 1987.

This is what makes the house a benchmark, beyond its wines: the idea, well ahead of its time, that wine was culture to be shown and not just goods to be sold. Today everyone talks about wine tourism. In Torgiano they were doing it fifty years ago, with the help of an art historian. Giorgio Lungarotti died in 1999; today the business is run by Chiara Lungarotti, while Teresa Severini works with her mother on the Foundation.

## What to taste first

A three-glass path, all Umbrian:

- **Rubesco**, Torgiano Rosso, bright ruby, the wine that opened the way;
- **Rubesco Riserva Vigna Monticchio**, Torgiano Rosso Riserva DOCG, deep ruby turning garnet with age: the wine to keep;
- **Torre di Giano**, Torgiano Bianco, straw yellow, so as not to forget that the 1968 DOC was white too.

Prices vary by vintage and channel: Rubesco sits among good everyday reds, Vigna Monticchio among serious riservas.

## How to visit

Visits to the Torgiano winery, with a walk of about half an hour through vineyards and cellars followed by a tasting, are by appointment and are booked from the [dedicated page](https://lungarotti.it/eng/torgiano-tour-tasting/). In November 2026 packages including a farm-stay night and museum entry started from 99 euros per person. The wine shop is open every day, on Sundays only in the morning. MUVIT is in the village, at Corso Vittorio Emanuele 31.

If we had to choose a single visit in this series for someone not yet in love with wine, it would be this one. The Torgiano museum does not talk about a winery: it talks about how people have drunk for millennia. Then, on the way out, the Rubesco is waiting.
