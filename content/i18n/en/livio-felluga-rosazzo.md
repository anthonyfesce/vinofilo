Long before appellations required it, a Friulian producer decided that anyone buying his wine should know where it came from. He did not write a set of rules: he drew a map. Since 1956 Livio Felluga's labels have carried a map of the Rosazzo hills, and it is hard to imagine a clearer statement of intent. This is the ninth portrait in the "Italian Excellence" series, devoted to a house that made the whites of Friuli an international benchmark.

## An Istrian on the hills of Rosazzo

Livio Felluga was born on 1 September 1914 in Isola d'Istria, then part of the Austro-Hungarian Empire. In the late 1930s he settled on the hills of Rosazzo, in the Colli Orientali del Friuli, a few kilometres from the Collio. In 1956, at Brazzano di Cormons, the company bearing his name was born, and with it the first bottles with the map on the label.

His work in those years was above all one of recovery: restoring old vineyards, building a mosaic of soils and exposures. When he died, in December 2016, aged 102, the papers called him "the patriarch of Friulian wine". He left his four children almost 180 hectares of hillside vineyards; today the estate has about 187 hectares under vine divided into more than 320 plots, according to figures published by L'Espresso in 2026.

## The map as an idea

The [house's](https://liviofelluga.it/) label has become an icon, but its importance is not graphic. In years when much Italian wine was sold under the name of a grape or a merchant, Felluga pointed to a precise place. It was an idea of territory before territory became a marketing word, and it influenced the way Friuli learned to present itself. For an overview of the region's great whites, see our [Collio and the great whites of Friuli](/en/articles/2026.09.23/collio-friuli-great-italian-white-wines/).

The wine that consecrated that idea is Terre Alte, born with the 1981 vintage. Originally it was mostly Friulano, then still called Tocai, with roughly a quarter each of Sauvignon and Pinot Bianco. A white from several grapes, designed to last, in a region used above all to single-variety whites. For its fortieth anniversary, Gambero Rosso's Vini d'Italia 2022 guide named Terre Alte 2018 white wine of the year.

## Rosazzo, a DOCG made to measure

In 2011 Rosazzo became a DOCG in its own right, after long being a subzone of the Colli Orientali. It is an appellation for whites only, and its rules seem written with exactly that kind of wine in mind: Friulano between 50 and 60%, Sauvignon between 20 and 30%, Chardonnay and Pinot Bianco together between 20 and 30%, with at most 10% Ribolla Gialla. The rules are published by the [Consorzio Friuli Colli Orientali e Ramandolo](https://www.colliorientali.com/). The heart of the zone is the abbey of Rosazzo, where the house farms the vineyards that give its other flagship wine, Rosazzo Abbazia di Rosazzo.

Here lies the reason Felluga is a benchmark: it showed that Friuli could make age-worthy, complex whites, able to stand beside Italy's great reds on restaurant lists. The criticism, if we want one, is that such a recognisable label risks counting for more than what is inside the bottle. But a Terre Alte from a mature vintage is an excellent antidote to that suspicion.

## What to taste first

The house's whites range from the pale straw of young wines to a deeper gold with age. A three-step path:

- a wine from the **Carta Geografica** line, to learn the basic style;
- **Terre Alte**, Rosazzo DOCG, the white that made the house's history;
- **Rosazzo Abbazia di Rosazzo**, from Friulano, Sauvignon, Pinot Bianco, Malvasia Istriana and Ribolla Gialla: L'Espresso gave a price of 85 euros for the 2021 vintage.

For the rest of the range prices vary by vintage and channel; the Carta Geografica wines sit among quality Friulian whites. Lovers of sweet wines will also find, in the Colli Orientali, Picolit, the area's rarity, which we write about in [Picolit](/en/articles/2026.12.18/picolit-friuli-sweet-wine/).

## How to visit

The house offers guided visits to the [abbey of Rosazzo](https://liviofelluga.it/rosazzo-abbey), with its vineyards and what it describes as the oldest ageing cellar in Friuli, followed by a tasting. Bookings are made by writing to the address given on the website. The company's headquarters is at Brazzano di Cormons.

Livio Felluga taught Friuli something we now take for granted: a wine is not just a grape, it is a place. He used a map to explain it. Seventy years later, that map is still the best way to read his wines.
