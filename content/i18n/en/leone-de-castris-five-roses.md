Some wines are born of a plan, others of an order that arrives out of the blue. Five Roses belongs to the second kind: a Salento rosé that, in 1943, an American general liked so much that it became a brand with an English name. This is the eighth portrait in the "Italian Excellence" series, devoted to a southern house that found its fame in wine's most underrated colour.

## A duke, a village, three and a half centuries

The story of [Leone de Castris](https://www.leonedecastris.com/en/company/history/) begins, according to the house, in 1665, when Duke Oronzo, of the family of the Counts of Lemos, bought land in Salento and founded the business where the winery stands today, in Salice Salentino. By the early nineteenth century the house's wine was already leaving, in bulk, for the United States, Germany and France.

The first break came in 1925, when Piero and Lisetta Leone de Castris began bottling: according to the sources consulted, theirs was the first winery in the area to put its own wine in bottle. A step that seems obvious today, but at the time meant changing trade, from suppliers to producers with a name.

## 1943, beer bottles and a general

The Five Roses story is the best in the series, and fortunately it is also well documented by the family. In 1943 Don Piero Leone de Castris made a rosé, to stand apart from the reds everyone produced. He had it tasted by Charles Poletti, an American general and supply officer of the Allied forces, who wanted tens of thousands of bottles. The problem was that there were no bottles: the north, where they were made, was cut off by the war. The solution was found in Brindisi, in the empty beer bottles left by American troops, sterilised and refilled.

The name comes from the Cinque Rose district, so called after a family tradition of five children per generation, and it became Five Roses because the first customer was American. The house presents it as the first Italian rosé to be bottled, a claim taken up by trade sources; to understand how a rosé is made and why its colour depends on time on the skins, see [how Italian rosés are made](/en/articles/2026.09.19/how-italian-rose-wine-is-made/).

## Salice Salentino and Negroamaro

The house's second pillar is red. The family's first Salice Salentino dates from 1954; the Salice Salentino DOC arrived in 1976, and for its Rosso and Rosato it requires at least 85% Negroamaro, with a Riserva that must age at least 24 months, six of them in wood. In the 1960s Salvatore Leone de Castris, a Cavaliere del Lavoro, expanded the business in Italy and abroad; since the late 1990s it has been led by Piernicola Leone de Castris, today with about 300 hectares, all in Puglia and all planted with regional varieties.

What makes the house a benchmark? Having believed in rosé when the rest of the world considered it a minor wine, and having done so with Negroamaro, the same grape that carries its reds. The broader picture of the region is in our [Puglia, Primitivo and Negroamaro](/en/articles/2026.09.22/puglia-wine-primitivo-negroamaro-nero-di-troia/).

## What to taste first

Three bottles to understand the house:

- **Five Roses**, a rosé from Negroamaro and Malvasia Nera, a bright coral pink; the **Anniversario** version comes from old bush vines and has been a separate selection since 1993;
- **Salice Salentino Riserva Donna Lisa**, deep ruby red, from vines up to seventy years old;
- one of the house's **Primitivo** wines, such as Per Lui, dedicated by Piernicola to his father Salvatore.

Prices vary by format, vintage and channel: Five Roses is an everyday rosé, Donna Lisa sits among serious Apulian riservas.

## How to visit

The winery is in Salice Salentino, at Via Senatore de Castris 26. Visits take in the historic offices, the barrel cellar and the museum devoted to Piero and Salvatore Leone de Castris, which gathers three centuries of company history; in November 2026 the standard tasting offered a Verdeca, Five Roses Anniversario and a Salice Salentino Riserva, with the option of adding lunch. The family also runs the Villa Donna Lisa hotel, with a restaurant. Information is on the [official website](https://www.leonedecastris.com/en/company/five-roses/).

Five Roses proves something Italian wine often forgets: sometimes the best story is not that of the oldest vineyard, but that of an idea that arrived at the right moment. A rosé in beer bottles, for a general far from home. More than eighty years later, it is still being drunk.
