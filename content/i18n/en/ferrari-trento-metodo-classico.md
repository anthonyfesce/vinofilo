The name has been misleading people for over a century, so let us clear it up at once: this has nothing to do with Maranello. In Trento, Ferrari is an ordinary surname, and the man who bore it built one of the few Italian sparkling wine houses that can be mentioned in Reims without lowering your voice. This is the first of ten portraits in the "Italian Excellence" series, devoted to the historic houses that gave Italian wine its shape. We begin with the traditional method, which found one of its earliest Italian laboratories in Trentino.

## A young man looking towards Épernay

[Giulio Ferrari](https://www.ferraritrento.com/en/history/) was born in 1879 and trained the way ambitious technicians trained back then: the agricultural school of San Michele all'Adige, then Geisenheim in Germany and Montpellier in France. In the cellars of Champagne he saw what he wanted to do at home. In 1902 he began making bottle-fermented sparkling wine in Trento, and to do it he bet on a grape almost nobody grew in Trentino: Chardonnay. The house remembers him as the first to plant it widely in Italy, and he certainly spread it through his own vine nursery.

The first recognition came early, a gold medal at the 1906 International Exhibition in Milan. But the most curious episode came forty years later. During the bombing of Trento, Ferrari had the entrance to his city cellar walled up; when he reopened it at the end of the war, he found bottles that had rested on their lees far longer than planned, and discovered that time had improved them. In the house's telling, this is where its reserve wines began.

## The wine merchant who went into debt

In 1952 Giulio Ferrari sold the business to Bruno Lunelli, who had opened a wine shop in Trento in 1927. Accounts of the time put the price at almost thirty million lire, an enormous sum for a cellar then producing around nine thousand bottles a year. Lunelli went into debt, but kept his promise not to lower the bar. Giulio Ferrari died in 1965, aged 86; four years later Bruno handed over to his children.

From then on, the story became a family one. Mauro Lunelli, a winemaker, signed the decisive moves: the first Rosé around 1969, the 1971 Brut de Brut now called Perlé, and above all an act of patience. In 1972 he set aside five thousand bottles of Chardonnay from the Maso Pianizza vineyard and left them to rest. They were released in 1980 bearing the founder's name: the first Giulio Ferrari Riserva del Fondatore. In 1981 the winery, by then moved to its Ravina site, passed one million bottles. Since 2011 the chairman has been Matteo Lunelli, of the third generation.

## Mountains, Chardonnay and an appellation of its own

What makes Ferrari a benchmark is not just its scale but the way it tied its name to an idea of place. In 1993 the Trento DOC was born, the first Italian appellation devoted exclusively to the traditional method, today promoted under the collective brand [Trentodoc](https://www.trentodoc.com/). The grapes come from mountain vineyards, where the gap between day and night temperatures preserves acidity and aroma: the foundation of a taut style, more vertical than opulent. We compared Italy's two sparkling capitals in [Franciacorta and Trento DOC](/en/articles/2026.10.26/franciacorta-vs-trento-doc-compared/), and Ferrari is the reason the comparison starts in Trento.

Then there is the public side, which will make some purists wince: the toast of Italy's 1982 World Cup winners, the years as "official toast" of Formula 1 (2021 to 2024, according to the house), the awards as sparkling wine producer of the year. The marketing is there, and it is muscular. But it holds up because behind it stands a range that ages well, something marketing alone cannot do.

## What to taste first

The colour is that of traditional-method Chardonnay: pale straw with golden glints that deepen over the years; the rosés range from onion skin to salmon. To understand the house, climb step by step:

- the non-vintage **Ferrari Brut**, the entry point, is the simplest way to taste the mountain style;
- **Perlé**, a vintage Blanc de Blancs, and **Perlé Nero**, from Pinot Nero, are the heart of the range;
- **Giulio Ferrari Riserva del Fondatore**, from Maso Pianizza Chardonnay, is the wine that explains why the house is compared with the great names of Champagne.

On prices it is honest to say they vary widely by vintage, format and shop: the Brut sits among good everyday sparkling wines, Perlé climbs higher, and Giulio Ferrari belongs to another category, that of bottles for an occasion.

## How to visit

Visits are booked on the [winery's website](https://www.ferraritrento.com/en/winery-tours/winery-visit/). In November 2026 the published list ran from 22 euros for the tour devoted to the Maximum wines to 85 euros for the great reserves tasting, with a 150-euro experience at the sixteenth-century Villa Margon, the group's representative seat. The winery is in Ravina, on the edge of Trento.

One question runs through the whole series: can a house be both large and rare? Ferrari answers with the numbers of a big house and with a wine, Giulio Ferrari, that comes from a single vineyard. It is a difficult balance, and that is exactly why it deserves to open the story.
