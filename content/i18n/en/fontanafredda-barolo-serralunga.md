Wine stories love kings, but kings usually limit themselves to drinking. At Fontanafredda, a king bought the land. In 1858 Victor Emmanuel II, then King of Sardinia and not yet King of Italy, acquired an estate at Serralunga d'Alba, in the Langhe, for Rosa Vercellana, the woman he loved and whom everyone called the *Bela Rosin*. This is the sixth portrait in the "Italian Excellence" series: a house where the history of Italy and the history of Barolo intertwined until they were hard to tell apart.

## The king and the Bela Rosin

Rosa Vercellana was born in Nice in 1833, the daughter of a soldier, and met the crown prince at fourteen, at Racconigi. Their relationship lasted a lifetime and was long a court scandal. The king made her Countess of Mirafiore and Fontanafredda; he married her in a religious ceremony in 1869 and in a civil one in Rome in 1877, a morganatic marriage that never made her queen. Rosa died in Turin in 1885.

The [house's official history](https://www.fontanafredda.it/storia/) records that the estate was registered to the couple's children, Vittoria and Emanuele Alberto. In 1866 the king also bought the first vineyard in the commune of Barolo, and from 1870 wines were made in the estate's cellars.

## The count who built a village

The real founder of the business was the son, Emanuele Alberto, Count of Mirafiore. In 1878 he launched production on more than three hundred hectares, worked by salaried, specialised labourers. Around the cellars he built a real village, with farmhouses, stables, a church, a school and a club, the Fratellanza Agricola Operaia. In 1886 the first Barolo left for abroad; in 1887, according to the house, Europe's first concrete vats were installed. At its peak, in 1918, two hundred people worked at Fontanafredda and more than forty families lived there.

Then phylloxera and the crisis of 1929 brought everything down. Between 1930 and 1931 the estate passed to the Monte dei Paschi di Siena bank, while the Mirafiore brand was sold to the Gancia family. The Tuscan bank would keep it for almost eighty years, and did not let it stand still: in 1964 Fontanafredda vinified a single vineyard separately for the first time, the Vigna La Rosa, long before crus became the norm in the Langhe. In 2008 the property passed to Oscar Farinetti, the founder of Eataly, with his partner Luca Baffigo: "after 77 years Fontanafredda is Piedmontese again", the house writes.

## La Rosa, the wood and the return of the crus

The [Vigna La Rosa](https://www.fontanafredda.it/vino/barolo-docg-vigna-la-rosa-2022-2021-2020-2018-2017/) is the heart of the estate: almost seven hectares in the Fontanafredda MGA (*menzione geografica aggiuntiva*, the official named vineyard area), one of Barolo's 181, on soils of silt, clay and sand.

In recent years the project has widened. From the 2019 vintage came the Barolo Vigna La Villa, from the Paiagallo MGA, and Vigna La Delizia, from the Lazzarito MGA; with 2022 came back Vigna Bianca, not bottled separately since 1985, Vigna San Pietro and Proprietà in Gallaretto. Since 2015 all the vineyards have been in organic conversion, with the first certified harvest in 2018: today the house declares 120 certified hectares. Around them lie the twelve hectares of the Bosco dei Pensieri, the "wood of thoughts", and, since 2024, hundreds of fruit and forest trees planted among the rows for biodiversity.

This is where Fontanafredda becomes a benchmark: not only for its size, but because it has managed to use its history without becoming its prisoner. Barolo, a DOCG since 1980 and today protected by the [Consorzio Barolo Barbaresco Alba Langhe e Dogliani](https://www.langhevini.it/), has lived through the cru revolution in recent decades; a house born as a large producer could have stayed on the sidelines, and instead became part of it.

## What to taste first

Barolo has the colour of Nebbiolo: a garnet that is not too dark, translucent, taking on orange glints with age. If the difference with neighbouring Barbaresco is unclear, here is a [quick guide](/en/articles/2026.10.13/barolo-vs-barbaresco-differences/). For Fontanafredda, one possible path:

- the **Barolo del Comune di Serralunga d'Alba**, to taste the character of the village, usually austere and long-lived;
- the **Barolo Vigna La Rosa**, the historic vineyard, to understand what a cru means;
- an **Alta Langa**, Piedmont's traditional-method sparkling wine, straw with golden glints: Fontanafredda made its first sparkling wine, Contessa Rosa, in 1959.

Prices vary by vintage and channel: the Serralunga Barolo sits among entry-level Barolos, the crus climb considerably, the riservas further still.

## How to visit

Visits go through the [Villaggio Narrante](https://www.villaggionarrante.it/), the hospitality project inaugurated in 2017. In November 2026 the list included the "Scopri Fontanafredda" visit with three wines at 35 euros, "Il Barolo di Serralunga" at 45, "L'Alta Langa" at 45 and "Le Vigne del Barolo", with three crus, at 50. The village also has a Michelin-starred restaurant, Guido Ristorante, the Osteria Disguido and a few guesthouses for sleeping among the vines.

Fontanafredda is the high point of this series when it comes to romance, and the risk was that the romance would be enough. It was not. The story of the king and the Bela Rosin brings visitors to Serralunga; it is the Vigna La Rosa and the rediscovered crus that persuade them to come back.
